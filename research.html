Research

How do you forecast nature when the weather that drives it can't be forecast far ahead? My research builds models that learn from many regions at once, start from each region's normal seasonal pattern, and are tested only on years they never saw.

Main research line · 2025–2026

Desertflow Global Bundle

Per-region forecasting of vegetation greenness (NDVI) across 133 WWF ecoregions and 22 years, using Google Earth Engine. A leakage-aware testing protocol checks for the common ways forecasting studies get inflated scores — features that secretly contain the answer, statistics shared across the train/test line, and leaky splits.

Research areas

Three connected pieces of work.

01

One method, five problems

The tree-routing engine, tested on vegetation, coral bleaching, wildfire, deforestation and PM2.5 air pollution. Published as a preprint on EarthArXiv.
02

Dryland vegetation

Desertflow: region-specialist models plus a router for NDVI across 133 ecoregions.
03

Honest testing

The leakage audit: a protocol for making sure a model is never tested on information it could not have had.

Research timeline

2024
Started independent remote-sensing research; first NDVI experiments using MODIS archives.
2025
Built the Desertflow core: per-region CatBoost specialists plus a Bayesian-ridge router.
2026
Scaled to 133 WWF ecoregions × 22 years, then took the method to five environmental problems: vegetation, coral bleaching, wildfire, deforestation and PM2.5.
November 2026
